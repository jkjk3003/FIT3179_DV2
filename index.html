Preserving the Past, Building the Future

An exploration of Australia's architectural heritage, contrasting the dense concentration of historical sites with the explosive sprawl of modern housing booms over the last century.

A data story by John Koh Jiakang, published October 2026. FIT3179 Data Visualisation.

1. The Geography of Australian Heritage

Australia is a vast continent, but its historical footprint is unevenly distributed. When adjusting for population, island states like Tasmania show a highly concentrated density of historical sites, recording the highest sites-per-100k ratios in the nation. Conversely, mainland states dedicate massive hectares of physical land to heritage conservation, prioritizing sprawling reserves over smaller, individual architectural sites.

2. A Timeline of Style

The expansion of Australian settlements during the 19th century—heavily influenced by British colonial rule—sparked a massive wave of Victorian and Gothic construction. The sites we successfully preserve today provide a direct archaeological record of these explosive, distinct construction periods.

3. The Modern Construction Boom

Transitioning from historical heritage to modern housing, data from 1983 onwards reveals severe, cyclical construction booms. The market shifted dramatically in the 21st century, with Victoria heavily outpacing traditional powerhouses to rapidly expand its urban sprawl.

About the data

  • Normalization. Density metrics are calculated per 100,000 residents to allow fair comparisons between highly populated mainland states and smaller island territories.
  • Geographic Bias. Over 40% of the Wikidata heritage points are heavily clustered around Melbourne, meaning architectural counts reflect Wikidata's specific coverage gaps rather than a perfect national census.
  • Housing Approvals. The modern building approvals dataset tracks raw monthly volume from 1983 onwards to capture modern economic cycles and market volatility.
