Preserving the Past, Building the Future

An exploration of Australia's architectural heritage and modern housing booms.

1. The Geography of Australian Heritage

Density and Protection

Australia is a vast continent, but its historical footprint is unevenly distributed. When adjusting for population, island states like Tasmania show a highly concentrated density of historical sites.

However, when looking at the physical land protected, mainland states like New South Wales dedicate massive hectares of land to heritage conservation.

State Rankings

When ranked by absolute volume, Victoria contains the most individual heritage sites. However, the deep blue gradient reveals that New South Wales leads significantly in total protected area, prioritizing massive historical reserves over numerous smaller sites.

2. A Timeline of Style

The expansion of Australian settlements during the 19th century—heavily influenced by British colonial rule—sparked a massive wave of Victorian and Gothic construction.

3. The Modern Construction Boom

State Comparisons

Transitioning from historical heritage to modern housing, data from 1983 onwards reveals cyclical construction booms. Victoria eventually outpaced New South Wales in raw housing approvals.

Housing Market Volatility

While some states maintain a steady rate of housing development, others experience massive economic booms and crashes. This interactive Jitter Plot reveals the true statistical distribution of monthly house approvals.

Each individual dot represents a single month of approvals. Tight, dense clusters indicate highly consistent development. Conversely, wide horizontal spreads highlight erratic markets prone to sudden spikes and dips.
